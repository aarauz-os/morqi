A mobile action adventure for the whole family. Children choose it for the hordes, the powers, the creatures and the story. The opportunity

Families spend on games. They don't trust most of them.

$3B+Player spending on the leading title in MorQi's core genre in under two and a half years.
~$20Monthly spend of the average paying user on the largest kids' gaming platform.
$520MThe largest penalty yet against a game publisher over dark patterns and children's privacy.

The highest-earning games collect their money through random paid rewards, endless limited-time offers and competition that rewards whoever spends most. Parents distrust those mechanics, and regulators now penalise them. Wellbeing apps for children teach worthwhile skills, but children rarely ask to open them. Nobody has built a game children choose on their own that also leaves them better off. That gap is MorQi.

The world

The Stillness

Qi has stopped flowing. Rivers stand frozen mid-wave, fires have turned to glass, and people have stopped feeling. Nothing has died; everything has simply stopped. The player keeps the last hearth that still burns, and wins the world back one region at a time.

A world frozen mid-motion

The world is a mirror. The Stillness outside is what being stuck feels like inside, and every region a player brings back stands for a habit they've built.

The Stillmaker

Precise, composed, perfectly dressed, and never once raising his voice. A grieving former master who decided that feeling is what makes people hurt each other, and is stopping the world to keep it safe.

Year one

Cold silver eyes and a silent bell. Every season, players thaw one more piece of what he froze.

The end of his story

Over the seasons the crystal falls away and his features soften into balance. The villain children feared becomes the proof that anyone can come back.

You cannot keep people safe by making them stop feeling.

The idea under every chapter, and the reason the story works for a seven-year-old with a temper and for a forty-year-old who has never cried at work. It also carries a mystery planned from its first line and answered over the first year: why is the player's hearth the only one still burning?

The characters

A master, a fox spirit, and five companions

Sorin

A master of qi, tai chi and feelings, earned through hard choices and living with what each one brought. He teaches one thing: keep striving to be the best version of yourself, all the way to your highest self.

Luma

The fox spirit who guards the last hearth. She grows a tail each season, and each tail returns a memory that unlocks the story's mystery.

Bond stories

Every companion has a personal story. Choices are remembered, and only the companions whose stories you walked stand with you in a season's finale.

Companions

Met in the story, never won from a random draw. Ranks climb from Stone through Jade, Gold and Spirit to Celestial.

Keepers

Every player is a Keeper, and every Keeper looks like someone

The player is the hero of the run, with their own spirit animal at their side. Keepers come in every skin tone, hair type and look, so every child can see themselves, and a player who sees themselves keeps coming back. The trim on a Keeper's robe takes the colour of their birth element, and Season Path outfits build on it from there.

At launch: a simple creator for skin tone, hair and face. Later: a full wardrobe earned and collected through the seasons.

The animal runs beside them in every fight, firing its own element at the creatures that element beats, and it grows over five stages into its mythic form, in its element's colours. The element chart

Every element beats two and is weak to two

The five elements, played like a card game everyone already understands. An element beats the one it keeps in check (water puts out fire) and the one that feeds it, which it uses up (fire burns wood). It does double damage to those two, very little to the two that beat it, and little to its own kind. A Keeper's own element comes from their birth year, so the same chart decides which creatures hit them hardest.

Learned by playing

The game says it out loud

Hits read “Super effective!” or “Resisted”. Every wave is announced with the elements that beat it, every power card shows what it beats, and the spirit animal aims at what its element beats first. By the end of Level 1 a seven-year-old picks powers by element without being told to.

The Path of the Five

Born one element, earning all five

A birth year gives every player two things, as in Chinese astrology: its animal becomes their spirit animal, and its last digit gives their own element. A child born in 2014 is a Wood Horse: a Wood Keeper with a Fire foal at their side. They start with their own element's powers and learn the others one at a time, in the order their element feeds them. Each new element beats the thing that threatens the one before it, so the lesson underneath is simple: what you nourish protects you.

Earned with time

One element at a time

A new element arrives about once a season, through play and a Teaching with Sorin, so wielding all five takes about a year. A newly learned element is an apprentice: its powers reach three stars until the next one arrives. In the demo, each level adds one.

A fighting chance

Jade Wards

When a shore favours elements that beat yours, frozen lanterns lean towards protection: Jade Wards that halve the harm from those elements, and more healing. The boss arena drops one when the Warden turns to an element that beats you.

Every second rewards

Built for the feeling of one more run

Every hit flashes, squashes and knocks back. Every kill shatters into crystal and drops a gem, and the pickup chime climbs as you chain them. Level-ups arrive every twelve to fifteen seconds as a choice of three cards, and a power never comes back once it is maxed. The first brute on each shore guards a Spirit Chest that spins a roulette over your build, so a chest always feels earned. All of it is earned in play; none of it is sold.

How it plays

Fast action outside, a home that grows inside

Win a region in a run→free its people→grow your hearth→raise companions and animals→take the next region

The run

Your Keeper and spirit animal against the Still. One thumb to move while powers fire on their own; hordes that grow with time and with your level; chests, boosters and an arena boss.

The Last Hearth

Home. Five halls around the fire, survivors with names and feelings, and a settlement that visibly grows.

Sorin's Teachings

Where breathing lives: short side missions of real qigong and breathing with Sorin, away from the action. Each one unlocks a permanent upgrade for the hearth.

The world map

Colour returns wherever you've won.

Walked, then opened

Nothing opens until a player has been shown how

Every area unlocks at the end of a short guided mission where the player does it themselves, with a hand pointing and everything else dimmed. The first fifteen minutes cover the hearth, the spirit animal, the first survivors, the first run and the first hall.

What it teaches

The lessons are in the rules

No screen ever announces a moral. Every lesson is a rule the player runs into or a consequence they feel. Children absorb it. Parents notice the change.

Trust and go to market

Built for the parent who pays

Trust and safety

A grown-up gate on every purchase and a monthly spending summary for parents. No open chat for under-13s. Parent controls, including the most a child can risk in Hearth Trials. No third-party advertising. Data practices designed for US children's privacy law (COPPA). Parent-owned accounts, deleted in one step.

Go to market

A family game for all ages with a neutral age check, since advertising aimed at children is tightly restricted and the parent is the one who pays. Paid acquisition aimed at parents and adults. Qigong, tai chi, wellness and parenting communities as channels. Each season's chapter as its own moment to bring players back.
